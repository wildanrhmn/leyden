Docs. How Leyden works.

Everything you need to pay, get paid and verify, without trusting a word of it.

Overview

Leyden swaps between the Zcash shielded pool and the Bitcoin Lightning Network, in both directions. Nothing on the way through touches a transparent address or a public Bitcoin transaction.

There is no account and nothing to connect or sign. You use wallets you already have:

  • A shielded Zcash wallet, such as Zashi or Zodl, with a unified address (u1…) or a Sapling one (zs1…).
  • Any Lightning wallet, such as Phoenix, Wallet of Satoshi or Breez.

Pay with ZEC

Pay any Lightning invoice from shielded ZEC. You send ZEC; the person you're paying receives sats.

  1. Paste the Lightning invoice. Leyden reads it on the spot and shows the amount, its description and when it expires.
  2. Add a refund address. Your own shielded address, used only if something goes wrong. Transparent t1… addresses are refused.
  3. Send the exact ZEC shown to the one-time address, by QR code or "Open in wallet". Every quote gets a new address, and the quote is valid for 20 minutes.
  4. Leyden pays the invoice after one confirmation, about a minute, and shows you the preimage: the cryptographic proof that the invoice was paid.

The invoice must include an amount and stay valid for at least 35 minutes, the quote's 20 minutes plus time for your payment to be mined.

Get paid in ZEC

Get paid by anyone with a Lightning wallet, straight into your shielded address.

  1. Enter the amount in sats and your shielded address. You can add a memo, up to 400 characters; it arrives encrypted.
  2. Share the invoice, as a QR code or a link that opens their Lightning wallet.
  3. The payment is held, not taken. Leyden sends your shielded ZEC first, and only then takes the payment.
  4. The ZEC shows up in your wallet after the next block, in about a minute.

If the ZEC can't be sent for any reason, Leyden never takes the payment. It stays locked until the hold expires, then goes back to the payer.

Lightning addresses

Claim a name and get an address like you@leydenpay.xyz. Anyone can pay it from any Lightning wallet, and every payment lands in your shielded address as ZEC. If the payer adds a comment, up to 200 characters, it arrives as an encrypted memo.

  • Names are 3 to 32 characters: lowercase letters, digits, - and _.
  • You get a manage key when you claim it. It's shown once, and it's the only way to delete the address later.
  • Each payment follows the same rules as Get paid: held, then the ZEC sent, then the payment taken.

Each swap is paid from the gateway's own balance on the other side, so the largest swap moves with what it holds. The app always shows the live limit before you commit to anything.

Refunds

When you pay with ZEC and the swap can't finish, the ZEC is sent back to your refund address automatically, with the memo Leyden refund, swap …. That happens when:

  • the Lightning payment fails,
  • you send less than the quote, or
  • your payment arrives after the quote expired.

The refund is what arrived minus the Zcash network fee for sending it back. Amounts too small to refund after that fee are held for manual review. When you get paid, no refund is needed: a held payment Leyden never takes goes back to the payer when the hold expires.

Leyden keeps no access logs, finds payments by scanning compact blocks itself rather than asking a server about transactions, and gives every quote a fresh address. The landing page compares a real bridge transaction with a real Leyden swap.

Trust

  • Paying with ZEC: Leyden holds your ZEC for about a minute, between confirmation and paying the invoice, the way an instant-swap service does. Failures are refunded automatically.
  • Getting paid: the hold invoice means a failure on Leyden's side can never cost the payer their sats.
  • Capped: swaps are limited to small amounts while this runs as a demo.
  • Lightning goes through a Spark wallet, whose operators see the gateway's Lightning payments but never the Zcash side.
  • Open source: a fully trustless version isn't possible with today's Zcash and Lightning, so anyone can run their own gateway instead.

Every claim here is checkable: the receipts let your browser verify real mainnet payments, and the run log lists every transaction.

API

The app is a thin client over a small JSON API on the same domain.

GET /api/inforate, fee, limits and live liquidity
POST /api/swaps/pay{invoice, refund_address} → a quote to pay with ZEC
POST /api/swaps/receive{payout_address, amount_sats, memo?} → a hold invoice to get paid
GET /api/swaps/{id}a swap's current state
POST /api/ln-address{name, payout_address} → claims a Lightning address
DELETE /api/ln-address{name, manage_key} → deletes it
 
curl -s https://leydenpay.xyz/api/swaps/receive \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"payout_address":"u1…","amount_sats":1000,"memo":"thanks"}'

A swap moves through these states:

Pay        awaiting_zec → paying_ln → complete      or refunding → refunded
Get paid   awaiting_ln  → paying_zec → settling_ln → complete

Run your own

Leyden is one Rust binary for the shielded wallet and the swaps, plus a small Node sidecar that speaks Lightning. You need Rust 1.89+ and Node 22+.

git clone https://github.com/wildanrhmn/leyden && cd leyden
cd lightning && npm ci && node init-wallet.mjs ~/.leyden/spark.mnemonic
cd ../gateway && cargo build --release
./target/release/leyden --zec-mnemonic ~/.leyden/zec.mnemonic serve --web ../web

The repository has the full setup, configuration and deployment files.
